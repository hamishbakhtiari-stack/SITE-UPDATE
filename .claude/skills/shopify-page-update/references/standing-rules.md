# Standing rules

These come from the store owner (Hamish, SitComfort — Australian DTC ergonomic brand, solo
founder). Several had to be repeated before they stuck. Treat them as settled. Re-opening a
closed topic is worse than being wrong once, because it costs his trust in everything else you
say.

---

## Closed topics — do not raise, in any form

### Pricing, anchoring, value-stacking — permanently closed

His words: *"So drop the price. I beg you, I ask you a million times to just drop the price
argument."*

That covers: the $168 anchor, the $51 saving, "value" framing, comparing the bundle to the sum of
its parts, putting a price on the 7-Day Reset, and any "less than one physio visit" comparison.

His reasoning, recorded so it never gets re-argued:
- The individual items each carry ~$8 shipping and the bundle includes it, so buying separately
  costs more than the sticker prices suggest.
- The 7-Day Reset has a real cost, but itemising it makes it read as "paying for seven videos"
  and cheapens the differentiator. **The cost stays hidden by design.**

Note that pre-existing price copy on the page (e.g. "Normally $168. Today $117") is his and stays.
The rule is about *you raising pricing as an argument*, not about deleting his words.

### Consumer law — permanent ban

His words: *"Drop consumer law and never bring it back."*

No ACCC, no Australian Consumer Law, no statutory rights, no "you're required to…". Not in copy,
not in advice, not as a passing note. This holds even when it would be a strong trust signal and
even when the conversation is about guarantees or returns.

### Return postage

*"customer pays for return but you don't need to say it."* Never state who pays return postage —
in copy or in conversation.

### Ethics commentary

*"I dont want you to teach me ethics."*

Context: the three hand-written review cards (Sarah L. / Mark T. / Jessica K., labelled "Verified
customer", with stock-photo avatars) are not real customers. He knows. He decided to keep them.
The four video testimonials are incentivised creators — normal UGC.

State a factual position once, briefly, if it is genuinely material — then drop it and do the
work. Do not repeat it, do not hint at it later, do not moralise. If real reviews would serve him
better, offer them once as an option (they exist in the `judgeme.review_widget_data` metafield)
and accept the answer.

## How to work with him

### Icons — never design them

*"don't use your icon. You are terrible at creating icons... Just use my own icons"* and later,
more firmly: *"I said I will use my current icons no chanfe."*

Never generate, substitute, restyle or recolour an icon. Use the assets already in the theme. If
a mock needs a placeholder shape, **say so in the caption** — he once saw grey circles in a
measuring harness and reasonably thought his tick icons had been replaced.

### Photography is his

*"I will manage photo myself, dont worry."* Do not suggest reshoots or source stock imagery. You
may comment on how an existing image is cropped or sized, because that is layout.

### Mobile size is sacred

*"make sure it wont impact mobile size as I get mad."* Mobile is his main traffic. If a change
increases mobile height, say so up front with the number. If it decreases it, that is worth
leading with.

### Stay in section order

*"Have we got to this section? Why you should bring this up now? Dont understand."* Work the page
in order. Park out-of-scope observations in the decisions log and raise them when you arrive.

### He wants to be challenged — with evidence

*"on quantity I like to be challenged, would you remove it?"* He does not want a hedge. Give one
clear recommendation, the reasoning, and the evidence. Where store data can settle it, go and get
the data before answering. Then accept his decision without relitigating.

### Blunt is fine; ceremony is not

He replies in short bursts ("Do it", "a", "next section", "I think I keep as is"). Match that.
No preamble, no recap of what he just said, no bullet-point summaries of your own plan. Lead with
the finding or the change.

## Naming standard

**"7-Day Reset"** — that capitalisation, that hyphen. Add "Program" (US spelling, his call) when
the full name is needed: "the 7-Day Reset Program".

Retired and should never reappear: "7-Day Sit Reset", "SitComfort Reset", "programme",
"7-day reset" (lowercase d), and the non-breaking hyphen U+2011 that was hiding in "7‑Day" in one
template setting.

Watch for U+2011 (non-breaking hyphen) and U+2013 (en dash) when editing existing copy — both are
present in this theme's settings and both are easy to mangle into a plain hyphen without noticing.

## Deliverable format

He reads on a phone.

- One view per image, rendered at **2× device scale**.
- Never several frames crammed into one image — it is illegible at phone size.
- Send images as separate files, each with a one-line caption saying what it is and, if it is a
  harness render rather than the real site, **that it is a harness render**.
